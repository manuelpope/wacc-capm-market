What it cannot do

This is the honest list. None of these are hidden behind a default, and none of them are fixed by the warnings.

The model

  1. It is not a full DCF. One FCF base growing at one rate, discounted at one rate. There is no working capital schedule, no capex plan, no debt schedule, no three-statement model and no mid-year convention.
  2. The normalization is an average, not an adjustment. The base is the mean of the last three fiscal years, which smooths the noise but does not correct for a known one-off — a restructuring, a year of heavy capex, a litigation charge. For a growing company the average sits below the latest year, so the model grows from a lower base than the trailing number would give. Three years is a short window for a cyclical business.
  3. g is an identity, not a forecast. ROE × retention says what growth the current returns can fund; it says nothing about what the market expects. It is also inflated by buybacks, which is why it usually hits the 5% ceiling.
  4. The caps are policy, not finance. The 5% ceiling and the 3% minimum spread move the answer. They exist to keep the perpetuity meaningful; they are not derived from anything.
  5. Beta is raw. Yahoo's 5-year monthly beta, with no Blume adjustment, no country risk, no size premium and no idiosyncratic risk adjustment. The equity risk premium is a fixed 5.00%, not estimated.
  6. The cost of debt is approximate. Interest expense over the latest total debt, not the average debt of the period. For a company that changed its debt level during the year the ratio is biased, and interest expense that nets against interest income understates the true cost.
  7. The capital structure is incomplete. Market cap plus total debt. Operating leases, pensions, minority interests, preferred stock and unfunded obligations are ignored.
  8. The tax rate is effective, not marginal. Trailing tax provision over trailing pre-tax income. Net operating losses, credits and deferred taxes are ignored.
  9. No currency conversion. Every figure stays in the reporting currency of the ticker. Comparing a US dollar DCF with a euro or yen one is not meaningful.
  10. No consensus estimates and no scenarios. There is no analyst forecast and no bull/base/bear case. There is a WACC x g grid, which is a sensitivity table and not a forecast: it moves the two inputs of the model and says nothing about what the business will do.

The data

  1. Yahoo Finance data is scraped, not contracted. yfinance reads non-public endpoints. Row names change, fields go missing and rate limiting happens. A missing field becomes a fallback with a warning, never a crash.
  2. TTM assumes four quarters. If a company has fewer quarters reported, the sum covers a shorter period, and the tool does count the quarters and say so — but the figure is still not a year.
  3. Value per share ignores future dilution. It uses the current share count; stock-based compensation and convertible instruments are not projected.

The application

  1. One local user, one file. No authentication, no multi-user isolation, no migration story beyond the CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS schema. The database is local and unencrypted.
  2. Only the latest analysis is kept. There is no valuation history per ticker, so you cannot see how the WACC moved over time.
  3. Manual growth is still constrained. A manual g is not subject to the 5% ceiling, but it is forced to keep the minimum spread below the WACC. The result reports that it was capped.

The analyst

  1. The analyst fallback is a model, not data. An LLM estimate is a plausible guess based on the sector and the company profile. It is labelled, range-checked and never allowed to overwrite Yahoo, but it is still a guess: treat every llm value as an assumption, not as a fact.
  2. The analyst cannot rescue a broken snapshot. If Yahoo Finance is unreachable, the request fails with an error instead of quietly serving an invented company. The fallback fills gaps, it does not replace the source.
  3. The analyst is slow. A first call pays the model load plus the inference — tens of seconds. The estimate is then cached with the snapshot, so the second call is free.
  4. The news are headlines, not articles. The model reads the title, the publisher, the date and a truncated summary. It does not read the full story, and Yahoo's own relevance ranking decides what reaches the feed.
  5. The news filter is textual. A story that mentions the company in one sentence is kept, and a story about the company that never says its name is dropped. The order of the headlines is a heuristic, not a judgement.

The grid

  1. The grid moves one thing at a time. It perturbs the WACC and the g of a simple model, so it inherits every weakness of that model, and it ignores the correlation between the two inputs: a WACC that falls because the business got safer usually comes with a growth rate that falls too, and the grid does not show that path.
  2. The fragile flag is a threshold, not a measurement. A cell is flagged when the WACC and g are less than 3% apart, because below that the Gordon denominator stops meaning anything. How bad the number is in that corner is left to the reader.
The one thing to remember

This tool tells you what a set of assumptions produces, and how much the answer moves when those assumptions move. It does not tell you that the assumptions are right. Read the methodology, read the warnings, and read the fragile corner of the grid before you believe a number.
