Sensitivity (WACC x g)

A single value per share hides the question that matters: how much of this number depends on the two assumptions that carry the most weight? The grid answers it.

How to read it

The grid recomputes the whole valuation for each combination of a WACC and a growth rate around the base case. It is nine by nine cells.

What you seeWhat it means
base case The outlined cell. It always matches the valuation shown above it, exactly.
green Above the current price. The intensity follows the distance.
red Below the current price.
dashed The WACC and g are less than 3% apart. The number is there, but the terminal value dominates it.
n/a The growth rate is not below the WACC, so the terminal value does not exist and no number is reported.

The axes

By default the axes are the base WACC and the base growth rate, plus or minus two percentage points in steps of 0.5. The size is set by two query parameters: 

GET /api/analysis/AAPL/sensitivity?span=0.02&step=0.005
The size of the grid is bounded

span has to be between 0.001 and 0.25 and step between 0.0005 and 0.05. The engine refuses a grid larger than 401 points per axis or 160,000 cells. Anything outside the bounds is answered with a 422 instead of being allowed to build a grid of millions of cells.

What the grid does not do

The grid does not apply the growth ceiling of the base case, because exploring assumptions above the ceiling is the point of a grid. It applies only the mathematical rule: a cell needs g < WACC to exist.

It also does not move anything else. Net debt, the share count and the base cash flow are held fixed, so what you are looking at is the sensitivity to the discount rate and the growth rate and nothing else.

What the grid is for

The grid is what makes the caps legible. The 5% ceiling and the 3% minimum spread are policy, and they are not visible as numbers in the base case. The grid shows that the whole optimistic corner of the table lives inside the fragile zone: a bull case built on a narrow spread is arithmetically large and economically weak.

For Apple the grid runs from $100.65 (WACC 12.43%, g 3%) to $698.88 (WACC 8.43%, g 7%) — a sevenfold range around a base case of $179.43 — and the two most optimistic corners are exactly the fragile ones.

The grid inherits every weakness of the model

It perturbs the WACC and the g of a simple model, so it inherits every weakness of that model. It ignores the correlation between the two inputs: a WACC that falls because the business got safer usually comes with a growth rate that falls too, and the grid does not show that path.

When there is no grid

A company whose cash flow is not positive has no numbers to show, and the grid says so instead of rendering an empty table. The same is true when the valuation itself could not be computed.
