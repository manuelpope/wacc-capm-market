The analyst fallback

When Yahoo Finance cannot supply a number, a local model is asked for the gap — and only the gap. Everything that keeps the answer honest is written down here.

What it may touch

Five fields, and only when the provider marked them as a fallback or as unavailable:

FieldAccepted rangeConsidered plausible
Pre-tax cost of debt0% – 30%0.5% – 20%
Effective tax rate0% – 50%3% – 45%
ROE−50% – 150%−30% – 100%
Retention ratio0% – 100%0% – 100%
Free cash flow margin−30% – 60%−10% – 45%

A value that Yahoo computed is never overwritten. The provider records the provenance of every field in field_sources, and that map is what decides whether a field is a gap.

What it may not do

  • It never estimates a scale. Market cap, revenue, the share count and the price always come from Yahoo. The cash flow is a margin the model provides, multiplied by the trailing revenue that Yahoo reported.
  • It never sees an unknown ticker. A ticker Yahoo does not know is a 404, not an invitation to invent a company.
  • It never replaces the source. If Yahoo Finance is unreachable, the request fails with an error instead of quietly serving an invented company.

How the answer is validated

  1. The model answers with JSON only, and every field it did not fill comes back as null.
  2. A value outside the accepted range is tried as a percentage: a whole number like 21 is normalized to 0.21 when the fraction is plausible.
  3. If it is still implausible, the value is discarded and the refusal is reported in the warnings. It is not silently dropped.
  4. A model that fails, times out or answers prose leaves the company exactly as it was.
How you know

Every value the model writes is labelled llm in field_sources, carries a warning naming the field and the model, and shows an LLM badge in the inputs table. The estimates live in their own table next to the snapshot, so a second request costs nothing and use_llm=false ignores them completely.

And when the model says no

A model that declines to fill a field is an answer too, and it is cached with the snapshot, so the model is not asked again on every request. A failure is not cached: the model may well be reachable on the next one.

The news

The headlines are the context the analyst reads before it estimates anything. A recent debt issue, a dividend decision, an acquisition or an outage changes what a sensible cost of debt or retention ratio looks like.

  • The feed is the Yahoo Finance news of the ticker, cached in SQLite with its own TTL of six hours.
  • Yahoo mixes market-wide stories into a ticker feed, so the stories that name the ticker or the company are ranked first and the ones that say nothing about the company are dropped when enough relevant ones are left.
  • The title, the publisher, the date and a truncated summary go into the prompt, and the model is asked to take them into account and to say in the note when a headline drove the estimate.
  • A short ticker such as T only matches as a word of its own, so "The market rallied" is not about AT&T.
  • A news failure never takes the valuation down. The news are context, not the analysis.
What the model said, once

On a live run over a recent outage story, the analyst answered: "recent outage news does not materially alter the cost of existing debt". That is the shape of a useful answer: a reason, attached to a field.

Turning it off

Three ways, and they all behave the same way:

# per request
curl "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/analysis/AAPL?use_llm=false"

# for the whole process
export WACC_LAB_LLM_ENABLED=0

# from the interface: the "Analyst model for missing data" switch

With the analyst off, the exact Yahoo values come back, the gaps fall back to their documented defaults, and the news are not loaded at all.
